Abstract
Multi-weather image restoration seeks to recover scene vis-
ibility under rainy, snowy, and hazy conditions, thereby
enhancing high-level vision tasks. Existing methods typi-
cally train on combined datasets with single-type weather
degradations, limiting their generalization to real-world
scenarios involving mixed degradations. Domain transla-
tion has emerged as a viable solution by generating di-
verse weather-degraded variants of the same scene. How-
ever, current approaches require separate models for each
degradation type, resulting in increased system complex-
ity. To address this, we propose DTMIR-Pro, a prompt-
based domain translation framework with latent space gen-
eralization for multi-weather image restoration. A single
trainable network performs multi-domain translation us-
ing domain-adaptive prompts and dynamic kernel selec-
tion via a proposed Dynamic Multi-Head Attention block
to learn diverse degradation patterns. The restoration net-
work takes translated outputs and employs a Multi-Weather
Fusion Block with global-local feature streams to cap-
ture complex degradations. Furthermore, we introduce a
Similarity-Based Encoder Routing mechanism to transfer
domain-specific features from the translation encoder to the
restoration stage. Extensive experiments on both synthetic
and real-world weather-degraded datasets demonstrate the
effectiveness and generalizability of the proposed method.

2. Related Works
2.1. Weather-specific Image Restoration
De-hazing:Numerous methods have been proposed for im-
age dehazing with [12] and without [18, 23, 25–27] prior
information. Yehet al.[54] used structural and statistical
feature learning for single-image dehazing. Liet al.[31]
addressed over/under-dehazing via channel and spatial at-
tention with skip connections. Fanet al.[14] proposed
a residual attention mechanism with cross-connections us-
ing paired depth maps. Recent unpaired learning ap-
proaches [43, 53] integrate depth-density priors to improve
restoration, though estimating accurate priors in dense haze
remains challenging. Furthermore, haze varies widely in
the real world, but existing methods often target limited or
common haze types, lacking generalizability to diverse un-
seen scenarios.
De-raining:Rain detection followed by removal has been
explored in [52], while Ahnet al.[1] introduced atten-
tive gating to balance rain removal. Dilated convolutions
with detail recovery were proposed in [11], and Zamiret
al.[57] developed a progressive multi-stage pipeline. Wang
et al.[49] used kernel-guided CNNs, and Huet al.[16]
incorporated depth to jointly handle rain streaks and fog.
Despite these advances, real-world scenarios often involve
complex veiling effects, which remain challenging.
De-snowing:Snow removal is particularly difficult due to
snow’s non-transparent nature. Zhenget al.[60] proposed a
multi-guided filter, while Wanget al.[48] used dictionary-
based decomposition. Zhanget al.[59] leveraged semantic
and depth priors, and Chenet al.[4] applied wavelet loss.
Recent works [45, 46] explored invertible networks and do-
main adaptation for improved realism.
While these methods achieve strong results for specific
degradations, they fail under complex weather conditions
involving multiple factors, such as veiling effects, limiting
their effectiveness in real-world multi-weather scenarios.

gle weather degradation such as haze [28, 44], rain [7, 58]
and snow [2, 36] removal. These single weather image

degradation, but the dire need of generalized algorithms
led the researchers to propose multi-weather image restora-
tion approaches [5, 20, 39, 62]. Valanarusuet al.[20]
equipped learnable weather queries in the transformer net-
work for decoder generalization. Chenet al.[5] distilled

2.3. Domain Translation and Prompt Learning
Various methods have been developed for image-to-image
domain translation. Early approaches, such as conditional
GANs, CycleGAN [61], and DiscoGAN [22], preserve
structural consistency using cycle-consistency loss. Star-
GAN [10] enables multi-domain translation using a single
generator with an auxiliary classifier. A recent method [40]
extends this idea for image restoration via multiple indepen-
dently trained domain translation networks, making the ap-
proach computationally expensive and cumbersome. While
these methods perform well in one-to-one mappings, they
struggle with efficiency and generalization in multi-domain
tasks due to the need for multiple generators. Single-
GAN [56] addresses this by sharing a single model across
domains. Building on this idea, we propose a generalizedmany-domain translation framework usingweather-coded
prompts, eliminating the need for separate domain-specific
networks [40].
Recent advances in visual prompts have shown promise
in vision tasks. PromptIR [42] applies prompts in decoder
networks for low-level tasks. In contrast, we introduce
visual prompts within the domain translation network to
generalize across input weather domains.Key distinctions
from [42] are discussed in the supplementary material.
3. Proposed Approach
In this section, we provide deep insight into the proposed
approach for multi-weather image restoration which in-
cludesa) Prompted Generalized Domain Translation(Fig-
ure 2), andb) Multi-Weather Image Restoration.
3.1. Prompted Generalized Weather Translation
Existing method [40] trained separate models for different
weather-translations, which is a cumbersome process and
has higher computational requirements. To avoid this, we
propose ageneralized domain translation solutionrequiring
a single trainable model to translate image from any domain
to its respective weather translated domains.
Given an input imageI, we obtain its corresponding do-
main translated images (I Haze ,IRain, andI Snow ). For this,
we use the prompted encoder based architecture for weather
translation as shown in Figure 2. The input (degraded) im-
age is initially passed through a convolution layer to con-
vert the image into features. These features are then further
passed through a series of Weather Coded Prompters and
downsampling operations to obtain the weather translation
specific prompts at various scales. These weather coded
prompts are then fused with the successive encoder features
to guide the domain translation stage. Further, these en-
coded features are passed through series of decoder blocks
to obtain the domain translated images in accordance to
weather coded prompts.
3.1.1. Weather Coded Prompts:
Input images may contain different weather degradations,
and translating such inputs to the target domains in a gen-
eralized manner requires feature adaptation. For enabling
multi-weather translation using a single generator network,
learnable weather prompts (P w) are fused with input fea-
tures to modulate them in accordance to the weather code
(w) provided. Please refer to Figure 3 for the illustration of
weather coded prompt block.Weather promptsare train-
able tensors that learn the arbitrary input domain informa-
tion while optimizing domain translation network. In order
to induce target specificity to the prompts, they are injected
with weather codes. For easing the input adaptability, we
make weather prompts attentively adapt to input weather
degradations. This is done by extracting global weights

3.2. Multi-Weather Restoration
Upon successful domain translation, the restoration stage
takes translated images as input along with outputs

(SBFR) (Sec. 3.2.1). Each domain translated image

catenation.

and transposed strided convolutions. At each level, MWF

3.2.1. Similarity Based Feature Routing
The domain translation network learns mappings from input
to target weather domains using weather-coded prompts.
Qualitative analysis (Fig. 7) and quantitative results (in
supplementary material) reveal that the learned features ef-
fectively suppress the input degradation while translating to
the target domain.We leverage these prompted features in
the restoration network to enhance performance.
To avoid redundancy from processing the input image, as
in [40], we proposesimilarity-based feature routing, which
guides the restoration network using encoder features from
the translation network. Given an input degraded imageI
and its domain-translated variants (I Haze ,IRain,ISnow ),
we compute the structural similarity (SSIM) betweenIand
each translated image. The translation yielding themini-
mum similarityis selected, implicitly indicating the domi-
nant degradation inI.
For example, ifIis a snowy image, its SSIM is typi-
cally lowest with the hazy variant, i.e.,I Haze . Thus, fea-
tures from the haze translation path (representing “snow
removal–haze addition”) are routed to the restoration net-
work. LetDT Edenote the last encoder layer of the domain
translation network; the routed featuresSBFR Oare com-

3.2.2. Multi-Weather Feature Fusion
In real world scenarios, the images captured in adverse
weather conditions may get affected by combined occur-
rence of multiple weather degradations (such as rain + veil,
snow + veil,etc). Hence, it is vital to effectively fuse the
individual weather features to learn their combined effect.
To achieve this, we propose a multi-weather feature fusion
module (MWFF). The schematic of MWFF is presented in
Figure 5. The main aim of MWFF is to fuse any pair of
weather degradations, both in global (semantic) level and
local level. The proposed MWFF consists of dedicated
blocks for global and local level fusion. In global level fu-
sion, the two weather features are passed throughFFT2d
→Conv 1×1→IFFT2dto capture global receptive field.
Further, for capturing the various sizes of weather degrada-
tions (such as varying rain and snow sizes), and to fuse in-
formation on a local-level, multi-scale gated depthwise con-
volutions are used (please see Figure 5). The multi-scale
gated depthwise convolutions are equipped with different
kernel sizes. Such global and local fusion of features en-
ables the restoration network to learn various combinations
of weather degradations.The effectiveness of each proposed
block is verified in ablation study (Sec. 4.5).

4.1. Training Details
We follow the training datasets used in [40] for training
the domain translation network, except the fact that [40]
trains separate networks for each domain translation, we
train our domain translation network in a unified manner.
After training the domain translation network, we freeze its
weights, and use its outputs for training the restoration net-
work. The domain translation network is trained for 100
epochs and restoration network is trained for 300 epochs.
While training the translation and restoration networks, the
initial learning rate is set to 10−3which is varied using co-
sine annealing strategy. The weights of both the networks
are updated on NVIDIA-GeForce GTX 1080 8GB GPU.
The discriminator network is same as in [56].

Similarity Based Feature Routing (SBFR):SBFR is pro-

network to provide input domain guidance and avoid re-

ity based feature routing really effective for multi-weather
image restoration?Experimentation results reported in Ta-

pared to no routing (viz. restoration only using the domain
translated images), and direct routing (viz. restoration using
the encoder features obtained using “all-weather” domain
code).Another ablation study on feature routing is provided
in supplementary material.
Dynamic Attention Block (DAB):DAB equipped with dy-
namic kernels is proposed to learn diverse attentive features
from the inputs. To analyse the performance of different
attention that can be utilized in encoders and decoders, we
perform various experiments. We use the traditional con-
volution attention, depthwise convolution based attention,
and the proposed dynamic attention. The results in Table 4
suggest that the proposed dynamic attention achieves better
restoration performance.
Multi-Weather Feature Fusion (MWF):MWF aims at
learning combinations of weather degradations beneficial
for image restoration in real-world scenarios. To scruti-
nizewhether the proposed MWF block is really favourable
towards effective restoration, we perform different experi-
ments. These experiments involve no feature fusion (direct
encoding ofF H,FR, andF S), additive fusion, concatena-
tion based fusion, local fusion, global fusion, and proposed
MWF. Results in Table 4 verify that the fusion of multi-
weather features using the proposed MWF turns out to be
more effective.Please see the supplementary material for

5. Applications of the Proposed Framework
Underwater Image Enhancement:Underwater images
exhibit diverse color degradations, making domain transla-
tion a feasible solution for restoration. We train a domain
translation network to convert colored underwater images
into varicolored variants, followed by a restoration network
trained on the UIEB dataset [30]. Qualitative results are
shown in Figure 8, and quantitative results on UIEB in Table
5. The proposed method outperforms existing approaches,
highlighting its potential for broader restoration tasks.
Restoration capability of any algorithm is utilized in
many higher-level computer vision tasks as a preprocessing
step.We have provided visual results on various such com-
puter vision applications in the supplementary material.
6. Conclusion
In this paper, we introduced a novel domain translation
with prompt based latent space generalization approach for
multi-weather image restoration. We proposed a novel
prompt based domain translation network for converting
the input image from any arbitrary weather domain to its
respective domain translated representatives. Dynamic at-
tention block is proposed for capturing diverse features for
effective domain translation and restoration. Further, we in-
troduced a novel similarity based feature routing approach
to collaborate the domain translation network with restora-
tion network. Finally, multi-weather fusion block is pro-
posed containing global fusion and local fusion branches
to learn mixed-weather degradations. Substantial experi-
ments on synthetic as well as real-world datasets demon-
strated the effectiveness of the proposed DTMIR-Pro for
multi-weather image restoration. We further demonstrated
the applicability of our approach on different applications.
