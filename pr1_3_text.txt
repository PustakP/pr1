=== PAGE 3 ===
Quick Recap: What’s Wrong With DTMIR-Pro Today
DTMIR-Pro (our baseline) works in two steps: it firsttranslatesa
degraded image into a haze-look, a rain-look, and a snow-look version,
thenrestoresthe clean image by routing only the single translated stream
that looksleast similar(arg min cosine similarity) to the degraded input.
Three weak points we identified:
1 The translation step is trained once and thenfrozen— it never
learns from how well the final restoration actually turns out.
2 It can only describe an image as100% haze, 100% rain, or 100%
snow— real photos are often a mix of these.
3 Itthrows away two out of threetranslated versions and keeps only
one, based on a rigid, non-trainable similarity rule (SBFR).

=== PAGE 6 ===
Change 1 — A “Fingerprint” Instead of a Label
What it replaces:DTMIR-Pro tags every image with a rigid one-hot
label: haze, rain,orsnow. Nothing in between.
What we’re doing instead:Give every image a flexible, learned
continuous description of its weather condition — more like a fingerprint
than a checkbox. This lets the network say “this photo is somewhat rainy
and somewhat hazy” instead of forcing a single choice.
Where the idea comes from:The BDG paper (ICLR’26) uses a similar
idea — instead of a fixed label for the type of damage in an image, it
builds a rich, learned description of the damage and feeds that into its
model. We’re borrowing that same philosophy for weather type.
Why it matters:Real bad-weather photos are almost never purely one
thing — a rainy evening often also looks hazy. A single label can’t capture
that; a fingerprint can.

=== PAGE 7 ===
Change 2 — Letting the Translator Learn From the
Outcome
What it replaces:In DTMIR-Pro, the part of the network that creates
the haze/rain/snow versions of the image is trainedoncein Stage 1, then
locked. The later restoration step can never tell it “you got that a bit
wrong, adjust.”
What we’re doing instead:Load the pretrained Stage-1 weights, but
keep that part unlocked during Stage-2 training so the quality of the final
restored photo sends gradient feedback backward to improve how the
translations are made.
Where the idea comes from:This isour own proposal. The reading-list
papers gave us the general direction (they all re-use feedback across stages
rather than trusting one frozen pass blindly), but none of them makes this
exact change, so we are not attributing it to a specific paper.
Why it matters:A part of the system that never learns from its own
downstream mistakes is wasted potential — this closes that loop.

=== PAGE 8 ===
Change 3 — Blending Instead of Picking One
What it replaces:DTMIR-Pro’s SBFR looks at the three translated
versions (haze/rain/snow look-alikes), measures which one looksleast like
the original photo (arg min similarity), and keepsonly that one. The other
two are thrown away completely.
What we’re doing instead:Keep all three, assign each one a learned
“how useful is this” weight via softmax, and blend them together instead
of picking a single winner. The soft blending layer itself isour own design.
Where the idea comes from:MIRAGE (ICLR’26) does not do stream
routing, but it does propose acovariance (SPD) similaritybetween feature
streams. That similarity idea is what we can borrow as therouting scorein
a later step; the blend layer around it is ours.
Why it matters:Throwing away two-thirds of your information on a rigid
rule is wasteful, and the rule itself has no way to improve during training.

Parameter Efficiency & GFLOPs Comparison
How We Restricted the Model to 3.93M Parameters:
Channel & Scale Calibration:Bounding the network width tobase channels=26
acrossnum scales=3keeps the multi-branch MWF encoder-decoder compact.
Prompt-Conditioned Weight Sharing:Instead of three separate networks for
Haze, Rain, and Snow, Stage 1 shares asingleencoder conditioned on a compact
weather prompt; translation decoders are bypassed during Stage-2 inference
(encode only).
Minimal-Overhead Additions:Unfreezing the translator (Change 2) adds0
parameters, while the continuous fingerprint MLP (Change 1) and soft-router
stem (Change 3) use global pooling and 1×1 projections (<0.04M added
params).

=== PAGE 14 ===
What We Learned & Immediate Next Steps
1. Fixing the Continuous Weather Fingerprint (B2 & B4):
Root Cause:Inone-hotmode, Stage 1 knows which target domain
(haze/rain/snow) to translate toward. Incontinuousmode without
supervision, the embedding MLP has no anchor, collapsing Stage-1
translation.
Immediate Fix:Add a lightweightauxiliary cross-entropy
supervision head(or initialize the continuous projection from the
one-hot embedding basis) during Stage-1 pretraining, plus testB1 +
B3(One-Hot + Trainable Translator + Soft Routing).
2. Scaling to Full Benchmark Datasets:
Bump schedule to the paper’s full100 Stage-1 / 300 Stage-2
epochson the complete 5,000-image SOTS dataset (targeting the
paper’s 36.92 dB / 0.981 reference) alongside realOutdoor-Rain
(ORD)andComprehensive Snow (CSD)datasets.
Run real-world unpaired evaluation (RTTS, RID, Snow-Realistic via
NIQE / BRISQUE).
